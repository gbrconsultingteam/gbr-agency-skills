# Consultation video script

The video sits where the banner image would be, at the very top of the funnel. It is the first thing a visitor sees, so its job is simple: let them meet the person, understand what the call is, and feel comfortable clicking the button.

Every one of these scripts drives to the same action, a booked consultation. That does not change by client or by business. What changes is who is watching and what the call is about.

## Ask for these before writing

Do not guess any of them. Getting the craft wrong is obvious to the client, and getting the name wrong is worse.

1. **Object of the funnel.** Services, training, or both. Both means two separate scripts in one doc.
2. **Type of business.** Permanent makeup, hairstyling, barbering, lashes, nails, aesthetics, and so on. This decides the vocabulary in beats 2 and 5.
3. **The person's name**, as they want to be introduced on camera.
4. **The business name**, as a customer would say it.
5. **The Drive folder** to upload the doc into.
6. **Language**, only if it is not English. Default to English for everything: scripts, headings, guidelines. Jonatan will say when a client needs Spanish.

If he has already given some of these in the thread, use them and ask only for the rest.

## The script structure

Seven beats, in this order. Around 110 to 130 words, which runs 45 to 60 seconds spoken. Hold that length: longer and people scroll past before the CTA, shorter and the call feels unexplained.

1. **Greeting.** "Hi, I'm [Name] from [Business]."
2. **Why they are here.** "If you're here, it means you're [thinking about X / interested in training with me]." Name the actual thing, in their words.
3. **How easy it is.** Click the button, choose a date and time, answer a few quick questions, you're all set.
4. **What the call costs them.** About 10 to 15 minutes, joinable from a phone. This removes the main objection, which is that a call is a big commitment.
5. **What happens on the call.** What the client will learn about them, then what they will walk the viewer through: options, what's included, pricing, what to expect.
6. **Gentle scarcity.** Set a reminder, the schedule fills up, spots are limited. Only if it is true.
7. **Close.** Click the button below to book, and a warm sign-off.

### What changes between services and training

| Beat | Services | Training |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | Thinking about the treatment, naming the specific services | Interested in training, whether brand new or already working and refining |
| 5 | Their goals, their features, which technique suits them, pricing, what to expect before and after the appointment | Their goals and experience level, the training options, what's included, pricing, what to expect from the course |
| 6 | Consultation spots are limited | Class spots are limited |
| 7 | "I look forward to meeting you soon" | "I look forward to speaking with you soon" |

Keep both scripts the same length and rhythm. They are siblings, and a visitor may see both.

### Worked example, permanent makeup, services

> Hi, I'm Adriana from Adriana's Beauty.
>
> If you're here, it means you're thinking about permanent makeup, whether that's your brows, your lips, or correcting work that was done somewhere else.
>
> Booking your free consultation is simple. Just click the button below, choose a date and time that works for you, answer a few quick questions, and you're all set.
>
> The call usually takes about 10 to 15 minutes, and you can join right from your phone.
>
> During our call, I'll learn more about your goals, look at your features and your skin type, and walk you through which technique will actually suit you, what's included, pricing, and what you can expect before and after your appointment.
>
> Please make sure to set a reminder, as my schedule fills up quickly and consultation spots are limited.
>
> Go ahead and click the button below to book your free consultation, and I look forward to meeting you soon.

Notice beat 2 names three real services rather than saying "our services", and beat 5 says "your skin type" because that is what a PMU artist actually assesses. For a hairstylist those become hair type, face shape and maintenance. That specificity is the whole difference between a script that sounds like the person and one that sounds like a template.

## Creating the doc

Build it as HTML and let Drive convert it. Two mistakes cost time the first time this was done, and there is usually no Google Docs editor connector available, which means a mistake cannot be fixed in place: it means another file in the client's folder.

- **Send raw HTML, never HTML-escaped.** Writing `&lt;h1&gt;` lands the literal tags in the document as visible text.
- **Leave out `<hr>`.** It converts into a stray row of dashes stuck to the following heading. Headings alone separate the sections.
- **Read the doc back** with Drive's read tool before reporting it as done, and check the headings and lists actually converted.
- If a bad draft does end up in the folder, say so and ask before trashing it. Do not quietly leave it there for the client to find.

Link the doc by title in the reply, and say it is in their folder.
